# Why These Rules Exist

ForgeExec's constraints are not philosophical preferences—they are load-bearing guarantees that prevent a specific class of failure. Without these rules, every vertical deployment becomes a negotiation: "Can we just add this one conditional?" "Can we cache this derived value?" "Can we share this utility?" These micro-decisions compound into architectural debt that makes the second vertical cost 80% as much as the first, the third cost 95%, and the fourth impossible. The rules exist because **reuse is won or lost at the boundary**, not in the core logic. If the executor knows about NLE, then HVAC becomes a fork, not a configuration. If adapters contain business logic, then every CRM integration becomes bespoke. If state is mutable, replay is impossible and audits are narratives instead of proofs. The constraints are not restrictions on what you can build—they are investments in building it once. Every "just this time" exception is a loan against future verticals, and the interest rate is 100%. These rules are how we avoid paying it.
